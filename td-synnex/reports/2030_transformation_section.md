## 12. A 2030 blueprint: preliminary recommendations for transforming the commercial organization

> **Interpretation, not sourced fact.** This section is a set of preliminary hypotheses built on the research above. It is meant to open the conversation with the leadership team, not to close it. Every target is illustrative and should be tested against TD SYNNEX's own data.

### The market TD SYNNEX will sell into in 2030

Six shifts from the research shape the recommendations.

- **Buying moves to software and agents.** Gartner expects AI agents to intermediate more than $15 trillion of B2B spend by 2028. Partners and end customers will increasingly send a procurement agent, not a person, to get a price, check stock and place an order.
- **Marketplaces become a main route for software.** Omdia forecasts $163B of software sold through hyperscaler marketplaces by 2030, with channel partners involved in about 59% of it. Distributors that are not wired into those marketplaces lose the transaction.
- **Vendors are picking fewer, bigger distributors.** HPE's May 2026 move to two global distributors is the template. Scale, data and coverage decide who stays on the list.
- **AI infrastructure changes the deal mix.** AI factories and Hyve programs bring very large, engineered, low-margin and cash-hungry deals. Selling them needs architects and supply chain planners more than account managers.
- **Value moves to services, lifecycle, financing and data.** Analysts rate resellers and distributors only "low to medium" for capturing the AI services opportunity directly. The opening for TD SYNNEX is to enable partners to capture it and to take a share of the value in platform, finance and data fees.
- **People still matter where the stakes are high.** Gartner expects 75% of B2B buyers to prefer human interaction in 2030. Agents will take the routine work. Humans will be expected on complex, high-value and relationship decisions.

### The 2030 thesis: from distributor to agent-ready orchestration platform

By 2030 the commercial organization should work as a platform that machines can transact with and people can build on. Routine buying runs touchless through partner portals, APIs and agents. Human sellers concentrate on complex solutions, AI infrastructure, new partner practices and vendor strategy. The company earns more of its gross profit from services, finance, platform and data, and less from moving boxes.

### What changes between 2026 and 2030

| Commercial activity | How it largely works in 2026 | What it should look like in 2030 |
|---|---|---|
| Order capture | Web portal, EDI and inside sales reps handle orders | Most run-rate orders arrive through APIs and partner or procurement agents, with no human touch |
| Quoting and configuration | Reps and specialists build quotes and bills of materials by hand for complex deals | A configure-price-quote agent drafts quotes and AI-factory bills of materials in minutes; specialists review and price exceptions |
| Pricing and special bids | Manual special-bid requests to vendors; rebate and price-protection claims processed in the back office | Agents assemble special bids, check vendor program rules and file rebate claims automatically, recovering leaked margin |
| Partner coverage | Coverage set by tier and region, with resources reallocated by hand | Coverage set dynamically by partner propensity and growth signals; long-tail partners served by AI account managers |
| Vendor marketing | Campaign execution and market development funds run program by program | Vendor demand generation run as an always-on AI service, with measurable pipeline sold back to vendors |
| Partner enablement | Destination AI, Practice Builder and events | "AI practice in a box" with agent templates, a services marketplace and a skills path for partners to sell AI services |
| Credit and financing | Credit decisions and terms set largely by rule and review | AI-driven credit risk and embedded financing offered at the point of quote, including as-a-service and consumption terms |
| Partner support | Contact centers and account teams answer order, stock and status questions | A 24/7 agent answers most order, stock, returns and status questions; humans handle escalations |
| Seller role | Many roles centered on order handling and account maintenance | Fewer, more senior roles: solution architects, practice builders, vendor strategists and key account leads |
| Main measures | Gross billings, gross margin, operating margin | Add touchless order share, gross profit per commercial employee, share of profit from services, finance and data, and partner AI-practice growth |

### Eight moves for the 2030 commercial organization

1. **Make TD SYNNEX the easiest distributor for an AI agent to buy from.**
   Expose catalog, real-time price, availability, configuration rules, credit and order status as machine-readable services that partner and procurement agents can call, using emerging standards such as the Model Context Protocol and agent-to-agent protocols. Ingram Micro has already launched a server for this. Being second is acceptable. Being absent from agent-led buying is not.
2. **Redesign sales coverage around three motions.**
   - *Digital and agent-served* for the long tail of the 150,000 partners, with AI account managers that recommend, quote and reorder.
   - *Hybrid* for growth partners, with a named human backed by AI tools.
   - *High-touch specialist teams* for strategic partners, AI factories, public sector and complex security and data center deals.
   Release the capacity freed by automation into solution and practice building rather than taking it all as cost.
3. **Build an AI-native deal desk.**
   Use agents to build quotes and AI-factory bills of materials, manage special bids against vendor program rules, and file rebate and price-protection claims. In a business that keeps about two cents of operating profit per dollar billed, a small reduction in margin leakage and quote cycle time is material.
4. **Turn vendor services into data and AI products.**
   Package sell-through insight, demand sensing, partner propensity scores and AI-run demand generation as paid services to vendors. This strengthens TD SYNNEX's case when vendors consolidate distributors, because it gives vendors something only a large distributor with broad data can offer.
5. **Help partners capture the AI services opportunity rather than competing with them.**
   Grow Destination AI into a full "AI practice in a box": reference architectures, agent templates, proof-of-concept environments with AI-factory capacity, a services marketplace in PartnerFirst, and financing for AI projects. Take a share of the value through platform, consumption and financing fees.
6. **Run Hyve and AI factories as a distinct commercial engine.**
   Hyperscalers and new GPU clouds need key account management, capacity contracts and tight working-capital discipline. Create a bridge between Hyve's design and manufacturing skills and the channel's enterprise and sovereign AI-factory demand. That combination is hard for competitors to copy.
7. **Make lifecycle and finance recurring.**
   Scale technology-as-a-service, consumption billing and IT asset recovery, using AI for credit risk and residual value. This shifts part of the profit pool from one-time product margin to recurring streams.
8. **Build the foundations first: data, platform, governance and people.**
   - A single view of partners, vendors and transactions across regions and legacy systems.
   - A governed AI platform, with policies on what agents can commit on price, credit and terms.
   - A reskilling path that moves sellers from order handling to solution and practice roles.
   - Published AI metrics. Ingram Micro reports about $1B a quarter through its AI assistant, and TD SYNNEX has no comparable public number.

### A phased path to 2030

| Phase | Timing | Focus | Signals of progress |
|---|---|---|---|
| Foundations | FY2027 | Unified partner and transaction data; agent-callable catalog, price and stock services; deal-desk and support copilots; set the AI metrics to report | Share of orders through digital and API channels; quote turnaround; first public AI metric |
| Scale | FY2028–FY2029 | Coverage model redesign; AI account managers for the long tail; automated special bids and rebate claims; AI practice in a box across regions | Touchless order share; gross profit per commercial employee; margin leakage recovered; partner AI practices launched |
| Platform | FY2030 | Paid data and AI products for vendors; embedded finance and consumption models at scale; Hyve-to-channel AI factory offers | Share of gross profit from services, finance, platform and data; vendor renewals as global distributor |

### Illustrative 2030 ambitions to test with the leadership team

These are starting hypotheses for discussion, not forecasts.

- A majority of run-rate transactional orders placed touchless, through portals, APIs or agents.
- Standard quotes returned in minutes; complex AI-factory quotes in hours rather than days.
- Gross profit per commercial employee materially higher than in FY2025, with headcount moved toward specialist roles.
- A rising share of gross profit from services, financing, platform and data rather than product margin alone.
- A partner support model where most routine questions are answered by an agent, with human escalation where it counts.
- A published AI performance metric, so investors and vendors can compare TD SYNNEX with Ingram Micro.

### Risks to manage

- **Agents can disintermediate distributors as well as serve them.** If vendors and hyperscalers build direct agent-to-agent buying, the distributor's role shrinks unless it provides credit, logistics, configuration and data that agents need.
- **Hyve's growth can crowd out investment.** It consumes cash and management attention, and its margins are lower. The commercial transformation needs protected funding.
- **Automation can damage partner relationships.** Long-tail partners still value a person they can call. A clumsy agent experience would push them to competitors.
- **Pricing and credit errors by agents are costly.** Clear limits on what an agent can commit, and human review on exceptions, are essential.
- **Many agentic AI projects fail.** Gartner expects more than 40% to be cancelled by the end of 2027. Start with measurable, narrow use cases.

### Questions to test the blueprint in the meeting

- Which part of the commercial organization would you automate first if you had to show a result in 12 months?
- How would you want a partner's AI agent to buy from you in 2028, and what should it never be allowed to do without a person?
- What share of your sellers' time today goes to work an agent could do, and where would you redeploy that time?
- Which vendor would pay for data or AI demand-generation services first, and what would they need to see?
- How do you connect Hyve's AI-factory capability to the enterprise and sovereign AI demand your partners are seeing?
