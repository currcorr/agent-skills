## 3. Segments (Note 11, p. 26-28; MD&A p. 35-40)

Structure: since Q1 FY26 the company has **five operating segments and four reportable segments**: Americas distribution (aggregating North America and Latin America distribution operating segments), Europe distribution, APJ distribution and Hyve Solutions (global). The CEO is the CODM and uses operating income to assess segments. Prior periods are recast (p. 26). Goodwill was reallocated on a relative fair value basis with no impairment (p. 9). Segment assets and capex are not reported (p. 26).

Definitions (p. 26, 31): distribution "bring[s] together a broad portfolio of IT hardware, software and systems"; Hyve "partners with technology companies to design, manufacture, and deliver traditional and accelerated compute, cloud, and connected infrastructure worldwide." Hyve has two offerings: **Manufacturing** (ODM and contract manufacturing) and **Supply Chain Services** (data center support, supply continuity, integrated supply chain orchestration) (p. 31).

### Gross vs net presentation effects (MD&A p. 35-40)
- Consolidated: more sales presented net, mainly in Hyve, **cut revenue growth by ~2 points (Q3) and ~4 points (9M)**; added ~11 bp (Q3) and ~19 bp (9M) to gross margin; added ~5 bp/6 bp (Q3) and 8 bp/9 bp (9M) to GAAP/non-GAAP operating margin (p. 35, 37, 38).
- Americas: more sales presented **gross** due to product mix **added ~12 points (Q3) and ~6 points (9M) to revenue growth**; cut operating margin ~22 bp and non-GAAP operating margin ~28 bp in Q3 (p. 36, 39).
- Europe: more gross presentation added ~4 points to revenue growth (Q3 and 9M); cut operating margin 7 bp (Q3) (p. 36, 39).
- APJ: more net presentation cut revenue growth ~4 points (Q3) and ~2 points (9M) (p. 36).
- **Hyve: more net presentation due to program mix cut revenue growth by ~66 points (Q3) and ~68 points (9M)**; added ~2 points to Hyve operating margin in all periods (p. 36, 40). This reconciles Hyve revenue growth (+52%) with the +117% gross-billings growth reported in the 8-K (not in 10-Q).

### Management's stated drivers by segment
- Consolidated revenue growth "driven by growth in both our Advanced Solutions and Endpoint Solutions distribution portfolios as well as growth in Hyve Solutions" (p. 35).
- Q3 gross margin decreased "primarily due to product mix in Hyve Solutions and in our Advanced Solutions portfolio, partially offset by improved margins in our Endpoint Solutions portfolio" (p. 37). 9M decrease "primarily due to product mix in Hyve Solutions" (p. 37). Derived decomposition: holding Q3 FY25 segment gross margins on Q3 FY26 segment mix gives 7.24%, so cross-segment mix is ~+2 bp; the -61 bp is almost all within-segment, and Americas alone (7.57% to 6.37%) accounts for about -57 bp.
- Americas: growth in Advanced and Endpoint; operating income up on revenue, offset by higher personnel and credit costs; decline in gross margin (p. 36, 38-39).
- Europe: growth in Endpoint and Advanced; Endpoint gross margin increased, Advanced gross margin decreased; higher personnel costs (p. 39).
- APJ: growth in both portfolios; Q3 gross margin decreased (p. 40).
- Hyve: "strong revenue growth in Manufacturing as well as Supply Chain Services"; operating margin up because of net presentation, partially offset by a decline in gross margin (p. 40).
- SG&A rose on higher personnel costs and higher credit costs (p. 37). Provision for doubtful accounts $32.6M in 9M FY26 vs $21.7M (p. 7).

## 6. Amazon warrant (Notes 2, 3 and 5; Part II Item 2) (p. 11, 14, 16, 47)

- Issued **May 30, 2026** to **Amazon.com NV Investment Holdings LLC**, described as "a customer"; reported on Form 8-K filed June 3, 2026 (p. 11, 14, 47).
- Up to **3,238,066 shares**: **215,871 vested immediately** at $0.01 exercise price; **3,022,195 vest in tranches at $191.10** per share upon "specified payment thresholds based on aggregate qualifying payments to the Company in consideration for qualifying sales transactions" (purchases of products and services). Expires **May 30, 2033**. Antidilution adjustments; no voting rights before exercise; participates in certain distributions (p. 14).
- The 10-Q does **not** tie the warrant to Hyve or name the products; thresholds are not disclosed.
- Fair value: $246.19 per immediately vested share; $116.15 per performance-vesting share (Black-Scholes: stock $246.22, strike $191.10, 7-year life, 4.29% rate, 33.13% volatility, 0.93% yield) (p. 14).
- Accounting: early-adopted ASU 2025-04 retrospectively in the quarter ended May 31, 2026; no prior-period impact (p. 11). An asset was recorded in other current and other long-term assets for the immediately vested shares; revenue is reduced as qualifying revenue is recognized, for both the asset amortization and unvested shares (p. 11). **Revenue reduction of $14.5M in Q3 FY26** (all of 9M) (p. 14); APIC "provision for common stock warrants" $65.7M in Q3 (p. 6).
- Derived: immediately vested value ~$53.1M; full vesting of the remaining tranches would imply ~$351M of grant-date value to be recognized as contra-revenue over the term; total potential shares equal ~4.1% of the 79.8M shares outstanding.
- Minor internal inconsistency: Note 2 says the warrant was issued "during the second quarter," but the equity statement records the whole $65.7M APIC increase in the three months ended Aug 31, 2026 (p. 6, 11).

## 12. Strategy and drivers in MD&A (p. 30-32)

- Self-description: "a Fortune 100 corporation and a leading global distributor, solutions aggregator, and original design and contract manufacturer" (p. 30). HQ Fremont, California and Clearwater, Florida (p. 8).
- Vision: "to be the vital solutions aggregator and orchestrator that connects the IT ecosystem" (p. 31).
- Five strategic imperatives (p. 31): (1) unify reach by expanding the portfolio in mature and developing markets; (2) target new customers via "specialist go-to-market and trusted advisor approach"; (3) expand addressable market through the vendor value proposition, "capitalizing on end-to-end capabilities to support business currently operated by vendors"; (4) diversify offerings, "including providing design, manufacturing and supply chain services to hyperscale computing customers"; (5) expand and attach service capabilities to engage earlier in the lifecycle, support complex deployments, drive renewals.
- Catalog includes "personal computing devices, mobile phones and accessories, cloud, security, data analytics, artificial intelligence ("AI") and hyperscale computing infrastructure"; customers include VARs, ISVs, corporate and government resellers, system integrators, direct marketers, retailers and MSPs (p. 31).
- Tariffs, VAT and similar charges are generally passed through in the sales price (p. 32).
- Economic and industry trends (p. 32): persistent inflation, elevated interest rates, market volatility, tariff uncertainty and geopolitical developments; demand depends on new products, replacement cycles, "trends toward cloud computing and AI." **Memory**: "Fluctuations in component pricing, particularly for memory components, may affect product costs, availability, and average selling prices. As AI applications and related data center investments continue to increase demand for memory-intensive configurations, the resulting changes in memory pricing may impact supply chains, product mix and pricing trends." **Hyve** "is highly dependent on the demand for cloud infrastructure, and the number of key customers and suppliers in the market."
- Forward-looking risk list includes concentration of sales to large customers, loss of significant OEM suppliers or customers, supplier finance program termination, Middle East conflicts (p. 30).
- Seasonality: historically slightly higher sales in the fourth fiscal quarter (p. 10).
