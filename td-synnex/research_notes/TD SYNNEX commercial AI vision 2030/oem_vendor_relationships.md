## Which vendors are the largest for TD SYNNEX, and what share of revenue do they represent (10-K)?

### Takeaway
Two vendors exceed the 10% disclosure threshold: Apple (12% of consolidated revenue in FY2025 and FY2024, 11% in FY2023) and HP Inc. (10% in FY2025, below 10% in FY2024 and FY2023). The 10-K names Apple, Cisco, Dell, HPE, HP, IBM, Lenovo, Microsoft and Samsung among its primary OEM suppliers. Fiscal year ends Nov 30.

### Cited Findings
- FY2025 (year ended Nov 30, 2025): Apple 12% and HP Inc. 10% of consolidated revenue. Apple was 12% in FY2024 and 11% in FY2023. HP was under 10% (shown as N/A) in FY2024 and FY2023 — [SEC FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1177394/000162828026003598/snx-20251130.htm) via search summary; consistent with the [FY2025 Annual Report to Shareholders](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1177394/000162828026007903/tdsnxfy25arsv2.pdf).
- FY2024 annual report: Apple was the largest vendor at 12% of consolidated revenue for the year ended Nov 30, 2024, up from 11% in 2023 — [FY2024 ARS](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1177394/000117739425000018/ars.pdf).
- The FY2024 filing lists Apple, Cisco, Dell, HPE, HP, IBM, Lenovo, Microsoft and Samsung among primary OEM suppliers — [FY2024 ARS](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1177394/000117739425000018/ars.pdf).
- Contract structure (FY2025 10-K): distribution agreements exist with most suppliers, including Apple and HP Inc. They are usually nonexclusive, territory-specific, generally short-term, subject to periodic renewal, and often terminable by either side without cause on relatively short notice. They generally do not bar selling competing products and do not require minimum quantities. On termination the supplier generally repurchases inventory TD SYNNEX holds — [FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1177394/000162828026003598/snx-20251130.htm); [FY2024 ARS](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1177394/000117739425000018/ars.pdf).
- Other financial context: FY2026 Q3 (ended Aug 31, 2026) revenue $21.6B, up 37.7%; non-GAAP gross billings $31.8B, up 40%; Q4 guide revenue $21.8B-$22.6B and gross billings about $31.9B +/- $0.5B — [TD SYNNEX Q3 FY26 press release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001177394/000162828026063314/ex991-fy26q3pressrelease.htm); [Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/td-synnex-reports-record-21-6-billion-q3-revenue-up-37-7-and-raises-quarterly-dividend/).
- Q2 FY2026 (ended May 31, 2026): gross billings $28.9B (+33%); Distribution $23.4B (+22%); Hyve $5.5B (+117%); Endpoint Solutions about +13% (higher PC ASPs and mid-single-digit units); Advanced Solutions +31% — [Q2 FY26 press release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001177394/000162828026045349/ex991-fy26q2pressrelease.htm); [Las Vegas Sun repost](https://lasvegassun.com/news/2026/jun/25/td-synnex-reports-record-fiscal-2026-second-quarte/).

### Inferences
- Apple plus HP Inc. is about 22% of revenue in FY2025. HP's crossing of the 10% line coincides with PC refresh and higher PC ASPs from memory pass-through (Endpoint is the large, low-margin end of the mix).
- The 10-K discloses only vendors above 10%. Dell, Lenovo, Cisco, Microsoft, HPE and NVIDIA are not quantified and are presumably below 10% each. Hyve's hyperscaler programs shift revenue toward customer-specific rather than vendor-specific concentration, so the vendor-share table may understate component-vendor dependence (e.g., NVIDIA GPUs, memory) in Hyve.
- Because Hyve revenue is growing faster than Distribution (Q2 gross billings +117% vs +22%), Apple's and HP's percentage of revenue will probably fall in FY2026 even if absolute dollars rise.

### Gaps
- I could not open the 10-K to confirm the exact vendor count (the brief assumed about 1,500-2,500), the Item 1A risk-factor wording on vendor dependence, or per-vendor shares below 10%.
- One search summary showed "HP at 100%" in a prior-year column; that is clearly an extraction error. Use the 10% / N/A reading.
- No disclosed breakdown of NVIDIA, Dell or Cisco share was found.

## What vendor-side services does TD SYNNEX monetize, and what are the economics (rebates, MDF, price protection)?

### Takeaway
The 10-K accounting describes vendor funds as reductions to cost of revenue/inventory (volume promotions, price protection, product rebates) and marketing/infrastructure reimbursements as an offset to SG&A, so vendor-funded programs show up in margin rather than as a separate services line. No explicit "services to vendors" revenue disclosure was found.

### Cited Findings
- Policy text (FY2021, FY2022, FY2024, FY2025 10-Ks, nearly identical): funds from OEM suppliers for volume promotion, price protection and product rebates are adjustments to cost of revenue and/or inventory carrying value; volume discounts under binding agreements are tracked program by program and reduce cost of revenue — [FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1177394/000162828026003598/snx-20251130.htm); [FY2024 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1177394/000117739425000009/snx-20241130.htm).
- Funds for specific marketing and infrastructure reimbursements, net of related costs, are recorded as adjustments to SG&A, with any excess recorded against cost of revenue — same filings.
- Vendor receivables are monitored quarterly and generally collected via vendor-authorized reductions to accounts payable — same filings.
- The 10-K does not use the term "market development funds" in the passages found (the phrase "marketing and infrastructure reimbursements" is used).
- Supplier agreements generally provide for inventory repurchase on termination (protects TD SYNNEX against stranded stock) — [FY2024 ARS](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1177394/000117739425000018/ars.pdf).
- Vendor-facing services observed in press: marketplace and cloud billing (StreamOne, Stellr with AWS Marketplace as Designated Seller of Record, Microsoft CSP), vendor-led enablement and training (VMware training centre, Cisco enablement), AI enablement (Destination AI), financing (Mark III deal), integration/configuration — sources above.

### Inferences
- Vendors fund programs mostly through price/rebate mechanisms, so price-protection exposure is highest in a falling-price period; the current memory price inflation is a favorable direction for inventory valuation but raises availability and working-capital risk.
- Data/insights monetization to vendors is plausible via PartnerFirst platform data, but no revenue figure or contract was found.

### Gaps
- No quantified vendor incentive amounts, MDF volumes, or percent of gross profit from vendor programs; no vendor-direct "data/insights" product evidence found.
- I did not retrieve Item 7 discussion of gross margin drivers.

## How are vendors shifting between direct, marketplace and distribution?

### Takeaway
Evidence points to vendors concentrating on fewer distributors (HPE, Broadcom/VMware, Microsoft Frontier tier) and moving cloud/software through marketplaces where distributors operate as Designated Seller of Record (AWS) rather than disintermediating them, though vendor-direct risk persists.

### Cited Findings
- HPE: consolidated worldwide distribution to two global distributors, regional/specialist distributors retained — [HPE press release](https://www.hpe.com/us/en/newsroom/press-release/2026/05/hpe-unifies-global-distribution-with-ingram-micro-and-td-synnex.html).
- Broadcom: invitation-only VCSP, ended white-label model, fewer authorised partners (concentration of the channel); in Asia-Pacific, distributors still run the Achievers programme where Broadcom does not — [Virtualization Review](https://virtualizationreview.com/articles/2025/09/23/broadcom-reshapes-vmware-partner-ecosystem-again.aspx); [CRN Asia](https://www.crnasia.com/news/2026/partners/broadcom-defends-vmware-partner-overhaul-as-apj-ecosystem-ma).
- AWS Marketplace transactions via distributor Stellr under Designated Seller of Record; AWS SCA includes making Marketplace programs easier for ISVs — [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250827433532/en/TD-SYNNEX-Signs-Strategic-Collaboration-Agreement-with-AWS-to-Accelerate-Cloud-and-AI-Adoption-Across-the-Americas).
- Microsoft: CSP authorization requiring distributor nomination and thresholds, and about 70% of incentives aimed at SMB-serving partners — [Pax8](https://www.pax8.com/blog/microsoft-csp-changes/).
- Hyve itself is a vendor-direct-adjacent business: it sells custom servers to hyperscalers and is a supplier relationship in the other direction — [Distribution Strategy](https://distributionstrategy.com/2026/04/td-synnex-growth-accelerates-as-ai-hyperscale-demand-fuel-distribution-and-hyve).
- Concentration risk language: agreements are nonexclusive, short-term and terminable without cause on short notice (10-K, above).

### Inferences
- Concentration risks: (1) Apple and HP Inc. at about 22% of revenue with short-notice termination; (2) NVIDIA and memory availability as an input risk for Hyve and Advanced Solutions; (3) hyperscaler customer concentration at Hyve with negative FCF; (4) two-distributor models mean share loss if a vendor swaps to the other global distributor.
- Vendor-direct threats are strongest where the product is digital (cloud, SaaS) and where hyperscalers, like NVIDIA and Dell, can sell AI infrastructure directly; the retrieved evidence shows vendors keeping distribution as scale/credit/logistics layer but not that direct sales are shrinking.
